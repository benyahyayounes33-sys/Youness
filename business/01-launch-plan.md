## 2. The key decisions (with my reasons)

### Samples or pre-orders first?
**Both, in this order: a few samples first, then pre-orders. Never stock.**
- Print **3 samples** first. You need to (a) check that the print quality is good before you take anyone's money, and (b) take **real photos**, which build much more trust than mockups 🔶. In Morocco, many buyers are wary of online shops that show no real product 🔶.
- Then sell by **pre-order**: you print **after** the order is confirmed (deposit or full payment, see section 7).

### How many designs?
**Show 10 designs digitally (free). Print only 3 as physical samples.**
Mockups cost nothing, so you can test 10 ideas. Physical samples cost money, so you only print the 3 that cover the most ground (dark image, typography, personalised).

### Which sizes and products first?
| Product | Priority | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **A3** | Launch | The size people picture as a "real poster" for a wall. Higher price, higher perceived value 🔶. |
| **A4** | Launch | Cheaper way in, fits standard frames, good for gifts and desks 🔶. |
| **A5** | Later, as a **set of 4** or an add-on | Low price, so little profit per unit. But 4 × A5 fit on one SRA3 sheet ✅ (with bleed). If the shop charges per sheet, a "mini set of 4" can be very profitable. Check this against real quotes. |
| **Adhesive** | Get prices now, test in **week 3 or later** | Different material, often a different machine, harder to print and cut cleanly. Some adhesives **pull off wall paint** when removed (ask for removable / "repositionnable"). Test it once you already have sales. |

### Six categories at once?
**I'm challenging this one.** Six categories with 400 MAD spreads you too thin. Every category gets at least one **digital** design (free), but only 3 samples are printed. **Let the reactions decide** (saves, DMs, orders). After 2 weeks, double down on the 2 categories that sell and drop the rest.

### Copyright and trademark rules (read before designing)
**❌ Never use**
- Logos and badges: car brands, football clubs, FRMF, FIFA, CAF/AFCON, film studios
- Real car **model names** in your product titles 🔶 (car makers own their logos and names ✅; some also protect the shape of their cars 🔶). So draw **fictional** cars.
- Film posters, film stills, film titles, characters, famous film quotes; anime and comics characters
- Album covers, photos of artists, portraits of real people (artists, players), lyrics
- Images found on Google or Pinterest, and **other sellers' designs**
- "In the style of [a living artist]"

**✅ Safe**
- Your own drawings and your own photos
- Invented cars, films and songs; landscapes and cities that you photographed or drew yourself 🔶
- Traditional proverbs 🔶; works whose author died **more than 70 years ago** ✅ (the copyright term in Morocco is generally the author's life + 70 years). Watch out: a modern photo or edition of an old work can still be protected.
- Fonts with a commercial licence: most Google Fonts use the SIL Open Font License, which allows commercial use ✅ (check each font's page)
- Stock elements (Canva and others) **only if** their licence allows products for sale. An unmodified stock element as the main design is generally not allowed. Read the licence.

**AI image tools 🔶:** check that the tool's terms allow commercial use. In many countries a purely AI-generated image gets little or no copyright protection, so competitors could copy it. Never put a brand, a character or an artist's name in your prompts. AI images are often too small for A3 at 300 DPI, so check the pixel size (section 8).

## 5. How to compare print shops

1. **Collect quotes** with the message (Day 1) and enter them in the **Quotes** tab. The tab turns "price per sheet" into "cost per poster" automatically.
2. **Shortlist** 2 or 3 shops: clear and quick answers, coated paper (couché) of 250 g or more, a guillotine (massicot), the ability to print full-bleed, and a test print allowed.
3. **Test print**: the **same** file (`print-test-sheet/print-test-sheet-A4-with-3mm-bleed.pdf`) on each shop's best matte paper. Use `…-no-bleed.pdf` if the shop doesn't handle bleed.
4. **Score out of 14** using the checklist printed on the sheet: deep blacks, smooth gradients, visible shadow detail, sharp small text, colours close to your screen, clean cut, no marks.
5. **Choose on quality first, then price**, as long as the price passes the Cost & Price check. A cheap print that looks cheap kills a "premium" brand.

## 6. Real cost and selling price

### The formula ✅
```
Cost per poster   = print price (per sheet ÷ posters per sheet, or per poster)
                  + cutting
                  + waste allowance   (🔶 10% of print cost: misprints, damage)
                  + transport to the shop ÷ posters collected per trip
Unit cost (1-poster order) = cost per poster + packaging for the order
Minimum safe price         = unit cost × 2.5          (🔶 rule of thumb)
Profit per poster          = selling price − unit cost
```

### 🧪 Hypothetical example (invented numbers, NOT Moroccan prices)
Suppose a shop charges 20 MAD for an SRA3 sheet and 2 MAD to cut it. One sheet = 2 × A4.

| Line | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Print + cutting per A4 | (20 + 2) ÷ 2 | 11.00 MAD |
| Waste allowance | 11 × 10% | 1.10 MAD |
| Transport | 20 MAD trip ÷ 10 posters | 2.00 MAD |
| Packaging | sleeve + card backing + envelope + card | 6.00 MAD |
| **Unit cost** | | **20.10 MAD** |
| Minimum safe price | 20.10 × 2.5 | 50.25 MAD |
| If sold at 59 MAD | 59 − 20.10 | **38.90 MAD profit (66%)** |

The **Cost & Price** tab does this for you. Type in your real numbers.

### Pricing rules
1. **Compare with the market** (the Competitors tab). If your minimum safe price is well above the market average, that size isn't viable with this shop: negotiate, change paper, change shop, or drop the size.
2. **Bundles, not discounts.** Packaging, transport and delivery are paid **per order**, so an order of 3 posters makes you much more per order than 3 separate orders. Offer *"3 posters for X MAD"* instead of "−20%". A 3-poster order costs 3 × the cost per poster + **one** packaging. Set X so that the bundle's profit is at least twice the profit of a single sale.
3. **Delivery:** free hand delivery in your city at launch (it costs you time, not money). Outside your city, charge delivery at real cost. **No free national delivery** until you know your costs and your refusal rate.
4. **Cash on delivery is risky.** 🧪 Using the numbers above: an A3 order refused at the door makes you lose the print, the packaging **and** the delivery fee. That can wipe out the profit of more than one sale. See section 7.

## 7. How not to lose money (and the order process)

**10 rules**
1. No stock: print only orders that are confirmed.
2. Personalised design (#8, names) = **full payment or a large deposit** (العربون) before printing 🔶, because you can't resell it to anyone else.
3. Outside your city: a deposit that at least covers delivery, then the rest on delivery. Confirm by **phone call** before shipping.
4. Never post a price before the Cost & Price tab says **OK**.
5. Batch your printing (3+ orders per trip) to fill the sheets and cut transport.
6. Send the customer a **photo of their printed poster** before shipping. It builds trust and catches mistakes.
7. No discount below the minimum safe price. Make gestures instead: a handwritten card, an A5 gift **only** on bundles.
8. No ads before your first 5 organic sales. Then a small test, paid from profits.
9. Log every dirham (Budget 400) and every order (Orders).
10. Keep the 50 MAD reserve untouched.

**Simple order process (WhatsApp)**
1. Customer writes → you reply with the catalogue + prices.
2. You collect: design, size, quantity, name, phone, city, address.
3. Payment according to rules 2 and 3 (bank transfer, cash-transfer agency, or cash on hand delivery).
4. You send a **summary** ("Order #003: Midnight Pass A3 × 1, X MAD, delivery on [date]") and the customer replies "OK".
5. Batch printing → photo to the customer → packaging → delivery.
6. After delivery: ask for a photo of the poster on their wall + a short review (your **real** testimonials).

## 8. Files: the basics for this week (full guide in a later step)

| Size | Trim size | Pixels at 300 DPI | With 3 mm bleed | Pixels with bleed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A5 | 148 × 210 mm | 1748 × 2480 | 154 × 216 mm | 1819 × 2551 |
| A4 | 210 × 297 mm | 2480 × 3508 | 216 × 303 mm | 2551 × 3579 |
| A3 | 297 × 420 mm | 3508 × 4961 | 303 × 426 mm | 3579 × 5031 |

- ✅ All A sizes have the same proportions, so **design once in A3**: the artwork shrinks to A4 and A5 without distortion. Export each size with its **own** 3 mm bleed, and check that small text is still readable in A5.
- Keep all text **at least 5 mm inside** the trim line (safe zone).
- **Bleed**: the background must extend 3 mm past the edge on every side, otherwise you'll get white lines after cutting. Confirm the bleed size with your shop.
- **Export**: PDF, images at 300 DPI, fonts embedded. Ask the shop whether they want RGB or CMYK. Canva: *Download → PDF Print → "Crop marks and bleed"* (CMYK export is a paid Canva option, and many digital shops accept RGB, so ask).
- Before sending, zoom to 100% on your PDF. If it's blurry on screen, it will be blurry on paper.
